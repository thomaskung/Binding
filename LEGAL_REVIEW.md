# LEGAL_REVIEW.md — Briefing for SG/HK Counsel: Points Economy & Adjacent Monetization

**Version 1.6** · Last updated 2026-07-30 · Revision history at the end of this document.

Not legal advice — this is a fact summary and question list to hand to actual Singapore/Hong Kong counsel. Nothing in BUSINESS.md, DESIGN.md, or MEMORY.md should be treated as a final compliance position until this review is complete. See those docs for full product context.

## The mechanism, as designed

1. **Points are earned** by job seekers via AI-verified actions (skill assessment pass, work-history verification) and automatically whenever their profile is revealed (accepted or declined by the candidate).
2. **Points are purchased with cash** by recruiters/enterprises (e.g. "$15-20 per Reveal Request," subscription bundles).
3. **Points redeem for**:
   - AI resume rewriting (JumpOnBoard's own service — narrow, single-purpose).
   - **AI career-assistant credit allowance** (formerly "AI-Credit Marketplace," scope-narrowed 2026-07-21): a classifier-gated, OpenAI-compatible API key scoped to career/JOB-related AI tasks only (resume rewriting, cover letters, interview prep, career-path guidance), metered by dollar-value cap against open-weight LLM inference JumpOnBoard pays for on the user's behalf. Funding is hybrid — points-funded free allowance plus a direct-cash top-up option. Enforcement (a moderation/classifier layer rejecting off-topic requests) is imperfect by nature, which is why counsel's confirmation should speak to whether the *intent and mechanism* of scoping is sufficient, not just the described policy.
   - **Training credits** (new, DESIGN.md §7a): a separate, one-way (points→credit only) instrument redeemable only for JumpOnBoard's own training content, also funded directly by quiz/course completion or enterprise cash purchase of staff seats (segregated from personal credits).
4. **Cross-party value flow**: in the reveal-override path, a recruiter pays points (cash-purchased) to reveal a candidate who hasn't opted in; if the candidate declines, the recruiter gets a partial refund, but the candidate is compensated in points regardless of outcome. Cash-origin value effectively moves from the recruiter's purchase to the candidate's balance via the platform.
5. **Terms of service** are drafted to state points are non-transferable and non-redeemable for cash or real-world vouchers.

## Why this needs specific review, not a general compliance pass

The intended design rationale (see BUSINESS.md §3/§11) is that a closed-loop, single-purpose points system — redeemable only for the issuer's own goods/services — commonly falls outside Singapore's Payment Services Act (e-money / stored value facility licensing) and Hong Kong's Money Service Operator / Stored Value Facility licensing regimes. Aspects of the current design that may stretch that premise:

- **Breadth of redemption (AI career-assistant allowance)**: "AI resume rewriting" is narrow and clearly JumpOnBoard's own service. The AI career-assistant allowance is scoped to career/JOB-related tasks (2026-07-21), narrower than the original general-purpose design, but the scoping is enforced by a classifier rather than by the API surface itself — worth confirming this is a real narrowing in counsel's eyes, not just described narrowing.
- **Cross-party value transfer**: the reveal-override compensation flow routes cash-purchased value from one user (recruiter) to another (candidate) via the platform, which may stress the "non-transferable" claim the ToS relies on.

## Specific questions for counsel

1. Under Singapore's Payment Services Act, does a points system redeemable for (a) AI resume rewriting and (b) the scoped, classifier-gated AI career-assistant allowance (item 3) still qualify for treatment outside e-money/stored-value-facility licensing? Does the career-task scoping (as opposed to the originally-designed fully-open API key) meaningfully change the analysis?
2. Under Hong Kong's AMLO/MSO regime and the Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, does the same mechanism trigger MSO licensing or SVF licensing, particularly given the cross-party value flow in item 4?
3. Does the reveal-override compensation mechanic (cash-purchased points → candidate compensated regardless of outcome) constitute a "transfer" that undermines the non-transferable ToS language, from a regulatory standpoint (as opposed to a contractual one)?
4. Given the AI career-assistant allowance is now scoped to career/JOB-related tasks (classifier-gated, not a fully-open API key), does this sit within the same narrow-redemption logic already relied on for AI resume rewriting? What enforcement or verification would counsel want to see that the scoping is genuinely narrow in practice, not merely described as narrow in policy — e.g., is classifier-based enforcement sufficient, or would counsel want harder technical restriction?
5. What specific ToS/consent language is required (beyond "non-transferable, non-cash-redeemable") to support whichever position counsel recommends?

Separately, and explicitly out of scope for the above: if JumpOnBoard ever pursues a genuinely open, general-purpose AI-infrastructure-reselling product (the shape the AI-Credit Marketplace originally had, before the 2026-07-21 scope-narrowing), that would need its own full review from scratch — it is a distinct, unscoped future business line, not a variant of the scoped feature these five questions cover.

## What's NOT in scope for this review

General PDPA/PDPO data-privacy review (candidate data, retention, cross-border transfer) is a separate, already-flagged item (BUSINESS.md §11, DESIGN.md §5) and can proceed on the lighter "private beta + parallel review" timeline. Part 1 above is scoped specifically to the points/tokenomics licensing question. **Exception (added 2026-07-20)**: the resume-first / data-monetization pivot generated a small set of *specific, discrete* questions (Part 2 below) that don't fit the lighter general-review sweep — two are stored-value/licensing (in this doc's original wheelhouse), two are targeted PDPA/PDPO questions about the aggregate-signal product. They are listed here concretely so counsel can answer them directly rather than folding them into the broad review.

## Part 2 — Resume-First Pivot & Data-Monetization (added 2026-07-20)

Context: DESIGN.md §2c-§2e and §7a-§7c, BUSINESS.md §1/§7. The product is pivoting to continuous AI resume maintenance (a persistent PII-bearing asset per user) and monetizing the resulting dataset **only in aggregate, non-identifiable form** — plus credit-based Benefits/loyalty and Training adjacencies. New questions:

6. **Aggregate signals as "personal data"**: If market-intelligence signals (in-demand skills, expected salary raises, hiring velocity) are computed only from **opt-in** profiles and only over cohorts of **≥ k distinct people** (proposed starting k≥20), with any below-threshold cohort suppressed entirely — do the published aggregates fall **outside** "personal data" under Singapore PDPA and Hong Kong PDPO? What minimum k (and what other conditions — e.g. added noise, quasi-identifier generalization) would counsel require to rely on that position in small/niche APAC verticals where re-identification risk is highest?

7. **Secondary-purpose consent for the signal product**: Is a **separate, explicit opt-in consent** (distinct from the AI-processing/redaction consent already captured at onboarding) sufficient to lawfully use a candidate's data to produce the saleable aggregates in Q6? What specific notice/consent language is required, and does the continuous-maintenance model (data kept and updated indefinitely for an active account) change the consent or retention analysis versus one-time ingest?

8. **Benefits/Loyalty discount-code model (reframed 2026-07-21, tier formula formalized 2026-07-23 — confirmation, not the original stored-value question)**: The Benefits/loyalty programme (BUSINESS.md §7, DESIGN.md §7b) no longer works as a credit rail. As now designed: tier eligibility is a **per-side cumulative counter** — seekers on lifetime points earned, recruiters/corporates on lifetime points spent, both monotonic historical sums, never a purchased or held balance of any kind; the benefit is a **generic, non-tracked discount code**; the user pays the vendor (flights, accommodation, IT equipment, healthcare, etc.) directly on the vendor's own payment page; JumpOnBoard never processes, holds, or forwards funds. The 2026-07-23 formalization (replacing a previously vaguer "activity/tenure-based" description) is intended to *strengthen* this analysis, not change it — please confirm that reading. (a) Please confirm this model — no stored value, no payment nexus — carries no SG Payment Services Act (e-money/SVF) or HK MSO/SVF Ordinance exposure. (b) Independent of stored-value status, does an affiliate/referral relationship with partner vendors (if any exists) trigger separate disclosure obligations? (c) Do consumer-protection or advertising-standards rules constrain how discount-percentage/savings claims may be presented? (d) Does branding this a "loyalty programme" trip jurisdiction-specific loyalty/rewards-programme rules independent of whether stored value exists? (e) Does a cross-jurisdiction "global loyalty programme" pull in additional regimes we should scope now?

9. **Contextual-ad consent posture**: For contextual-only advertising (targeted by page/role/skill context, **no** behavioral or individual-level tracking, no PII disclosed to advertisers — DESIGN.md §7c), what consent/notice posture is required under PDPA/PDPO? And if any *cohort-level* ad targeting reuses the Q6 k-anonymity threshold, does that change the answer?

10. **Training credit redemption (new, confirmation-only from the start)**: Training credits (DESIGN.md §7a) are a separate, narrow, one-way (points→credit only) instrument redeemable only for JumpOnBoard's own training content, with three funding paths: points-conversion, direct quiz/course earning, and **enterprise cash purchase of staff seats** (segregated from personal credits, never commingled). Please confirm this narrow, same-service redemption doesn't require SVF/PSA treatment — flagging the enterprise cash-purchase path specifically: cash-in → held credit balance → later redemption is the classic prepaid/stored-value trigger pattern, and narrow single-purpose redemption is the defense being relied on, but counsel should evaluate that specific funding path on its own rather than have it folded anonymously under the broader "segregated-balance" description.

## Part 3 — Recruiter Monetization (added 2026-07-22)

Context: BUSINESS.md §7, DESIGN.md §4a. Recruiter-side monetization now includes a recruiter-initiated override "take-back": a recruiter can cancel a pending override before the candidate responds, forfeiting the charge (not refunded) to the candidate as compensation, in addition to the per-role dynamic reveal pricing, spend budgeting, and paid AI JD-assist described in those sections (none of which raise a distinct tokenomics question beyond what's already covered above — they change *how much* is spent and *when*, not the closed-loop/non-redeemable nature of the points themselves).

11. **Recruiter-initiated override reversal — forfeited charge paid to candidate**: This is a variant of the cross-party value flow already flagged under Q3 (cash-purchased points moving from recruiter to candidate via the platform), now triggered by the recruiter's own cancellation rather than the candidate's decline. Product rationale as stated by the founder (not a legal conclusion): the points system is non-monetary and non-redeemable outside the platform — positioned as closer to a coupon/airline-miles model than a payment instrument — so a point movement between two platform participants is not, in the product's design intent, a cash-equivalent transfer. Please confirm whether this new trigger (recruiter-initiated cancellation, as opposed to candidate decline, which Q3 already covers) changes the regulatory analysis, or whether it should simply be assessed together with Q3 as the same underlying "cash-origin value moves from recruiter to candidate via the platform" pattern.

## Part 4 — External Loyalty Partner Bridge (added 2026-07-23, far roadmap, no active initiative)

Context: BUSINESS.md §7, DESIGN.md §7d. A directional idea only — no partner has been approached. Prompted by researching OpenRice's Asia Miles integration; corrects an initial assumption that OpenRice converts its own points into Asia Miles — it does not (the two ledgers are separate, per OpenRice's own FAQ, and never transfer either way). The actual mechanism is **dual-crediting**: Cathay Pacific independently credits its own Asia Miles for qualifying OpenRice actions, without touching OpenRice's own points. J.O.B.'s design follows the same shape and is split into two triggers that sit in **different legal buckets** — kept as two separate questions below, not one, because the secondary-source research supporting Q12 does not extend to Q13.

**Directional research already done (secondary sources — law-firm commentary and public HKMA/MAS guidance summaries; primary statute/PDF fetches were blocked, so this is informal, not verified against primary text)**: Hong Kong's Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance exempts single-purpose SVFs outright (Schedule 8), and commentary indicates multi-purpose bonus-point/airline-mileage schemes can also be exempt where non-cash and not marketed as payment — Asia Miles itself operates unlicensed across 200+ unrelated HK merchants on this basis. Singapore's Payment Services Act excludes "limited purpose e-money"/"limited purpose digital payment token," and MAS's own published guidance names airline frequent-flyer miles as the textbook example, with the marketing-as-loyalty-not-payment framing as a key factor.

12. **Activity-based partner crediting (future, infrastructure-gated — a successful placement or reveal-accept independently credited by a partner in its own currency)**: Given the research summary above, please confirm (a) this structure — J.O.B. points never becoming convertible into the partner's currency, the partner independently crediting its own currency for a verified J.O.B. event, no exchange rate ever existing between the two — falls within the same exemption logic already relied on for the points system generally; (b) what would disqualify it from that exemption (e.g. if the partnership were structured or marketed as a payment/exchange service rather than a loyalty coalition, or if J.O.B. points ever became convertible outward); (c) whether the analysis differs meaningfully between the HK and SG regimes for this specific shape. Not to be actioned before this analysis is confirmed and before any real partner agreement exists.

13. **Subscription-based partner crediting (near-term concept — Pro seekers/paid recruiter tiers earn partner miles on each renewed subscription period)**: This is deliberately **not** assumed to be covered by Q12's analysis — awarding miles as a bonus for paying a subscription is structurally closer to a card-rewards pattern (miles-for-cash-spend, e.g. Amex Membership Rewards) than to the activity-based loyalty exemption above. Please advise: (a) whether this shape carries different SVF/PSA exposure than Q12's activity-based trigger; (b) whether framing it as a "subscription bonus" versus a "loyalty reward" changes the analysis under either regime's marketed-as-loyalty test; (c) whether it risks being characterized as a rebate-on-spend or a promotional payment-inducement subject to separate consumer-protection/advertising rules; (d) the cross-border PDPA/PDPO implications of sharing a subscriber's identity data (name, email, or partner-loyalty-account number) with a foreign partner to credit them, independent of the stored-value question. Not to be actioned before this analysis is confirmed and before any real partner agreement exists.

## Part 5 — PDPA/PDPO Profiling, Residency & Internal Access (added 2026-07-28)

Context: the PROBLEM.md remediation pass and the Edge/Core layered-privacy architecture (DESIGN.md §2f, BUSINESS.md §8/§9a). Note for counsel on Part 1's scope: the residual cross-party value-flow concern raised by fiat top-ups (planned) plus candidate compensation is already covered by Q3/Q11 — no new tokenomics question arises from this pass.

14. **Granular consent for automated recruitment profiling**: consent is now captured as three distinct grants (migration 0016): (a) AI processing/redaction and (b) automated AI matching/profiling — both **required** to use the service; (c) continuous AI resume maintenance — **optional at onboarding, enable-able just-in-time at the point of use, and withdrawable any time** via a settings toggle (withdrawal clears the consent timestamp and the maintenance loop refuses to run). Under Singapore's PDPA (including the 2024 updates on automated decision-making notification) and Hong Kong PCPD guidance: (i) is this granularity sufficient notification/consent for automated profiling that significantly affects employment prospects? (ii) is the required-vs-optional split right — i.e., may processing + profiling legitimately be conditions of service while maintenance must remain declinable? (iii) what exact notice/consent language should each checkbox carry?

15. **Accuracy Obligation via suggest-and-approve**: the AI never commits a profile change autonomously — every AI-drafted update (onboarding extraction, maintenance drafts) is presented for explicit user approval before it enters the matchable profile, and the AI-never-fabricates rule bars inventing facts (DESIGN.md §2c). Does this human-in-the-loop commit structure satisfy PDPA's Accuracy Obligation (and the PDPO DPP2 analogue) for data used in consequential hiring decisions, on the theory that the user's approval vouches for accuracy? What audit evidence would counsel want retained (we keep an append-only cross-party disclosure log — migration 0017)?

16. **Interim US/APAC processing of raw resume text**: AI redaction of raw (pre-redaction) resume text runs on Modal serverless GPUs, now region-pinned to Asia-Pacific (previously implicit US default). Until the in-jurisdiction edge migration (DESIGN.md §2f): what DPA/model-contractual-clause language with Modal (and Supabase/Vercel — subprocessor register at `/privacy`; Vercel replaced Cloudflare as hosting subprocessor 2026-07-29) satisfies PDPA §26 comparable-protection and PDPO s.33 posture for this processing?

17. **Edge/Core inter-opco transfers (target architecture)**: under the planned split — HK edge (HK opco) holding raw PII, SG core (SG opco) receiving only pseudonymized text + vectors for matching — please confirm (a) an inter-opco DPA + PDPO s.33 posture suffices for the HK-edge→SG-core flow; (b) the same shape works for an enterprise client-VPC edge → SG core flow under a client DPA; (c) our framing that the core remains fully within PDPA/PDPO scope because pseudonymized-is-not-anonymized is the correct legal posture (we deliberately claim no anonymity exemption).

18. **Internal staff access (future ops panel + outsourced TA service)**: design (DESIGN.md §2f Layer 3): support staff see masked-by-default PII with break-glass unmask (reason required, time-boxed, logged append-only); staff delivering an outsourced talent-acquisition service operate strictly as *internal recruiters* — same pseudonymized surface, same reveal economics/caps/audit as any external recruiter, with a hard rule that the TA role never combines with ops-admin privileges. (a) Does the candidate's existing recruiter-matching consent cover the TA-service case (platform staff as recruiter-of-record), or is a distinct notice/consent needed? (b) Does masked-by-default + break-glass-with-reason + append-only audit satisfy PDPA/PDPO accountability and security obligations for intra-organization access to personal data?
